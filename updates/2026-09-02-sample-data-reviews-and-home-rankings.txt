Sample data

_sample_data.py holds the dataset and is shared by the seed commands; the
leading underscore keeps Django's command discovery from treating it as a
command. 15 restaurants around Northern Mindanao with real coordinates
(Iligan, Cagayan de Oro, Opol, Ozamiz, Malaybalay) - real ones so distance
maths has something meaningful to sort by. Each gets an owner account, a
location row, a 7-day opening template, cuisine categories, sometimes a
branch, and 6 dishes. Plus 8 diner accounts to review them.

  python manage.py seed_sample_data [--fresh]

Idempotent on natural keys (restaurant email, account username, dish name),
so re-running adds only what is missing. --fresh deletes the sample rows
first and nothing else. Every sample login uses the password goodfood123 -
see the note at the bottom about that.

Result: 15 new restaurants (16 total with the pre-existing one), 90 new
dishes, all 16 restaurants carrying at least 5 dishes.

Blocked on a schema drift first: restaurant_operating_hours.opening_time
and closing_time were still NOT NULL in the live database, so a restaurant
closed on a Sunday could not be seeded. The model has said null=True since
0001_initial and the deleted reconcile_existing_db.sql was supposed to have
dropped it. Migration 0002 does it. It has to be RunSQL - an AlterField
emits nothing, because Django compares model state and the state already
matched. Worth remembering: makemigrations will not catch drift of this
shape.


Reviews and ratings

review/queries.py, same flat style as the rest:

  getRestaurantReviews / getDishReviews
      -> {reviews, summary, my_review}. summary carries the averages plus a
      stars_5..stars_1 histogram via COUNT(*) FILTER, so the screen draws
      its bars without a second round trip. my_review is the signed-in
      diner's own, which is what lets the form open pre-filled.
  addRestaurantReview   food / service / ambiance, each 1-5; overall is
                        their mean unless one is passed explicitly
  addDishReview         a single rating
  deleteRestaurantReview / deleteDishReview
  getMyReviews          a diner's own history
  getOwnerReviews       restaurant + dish reviews on the owner's restaurant,
                        UNION ALL'd and tagged review_kind

Both add* functions UPSERT - one review per account per subject. A second
submission edits the first rather than stacking, which is why the form is
"Post" or "Update" depending on what came back. Deletes are scoped to the
author, so a diner can only remove their own.

Posting a review also INSERTs the owner's notification, on the same cursor
and transaction as the review itself, rather than as a second call. Skipped
when an owner reviews their own restaurant.

Front end: WriteReviewScreen is one page for both kinds, switched on
route.params.mode. A restaurant gets three criteria and a live overall
average; a dish gets one rating. Both take an optional comment. Reached
from DinerRestaurantScreen (restaurant profile, menu with per-dish ratings,
hours, reviews) and DinerDishScreen. Owners read theirs at OwnerReviewsScreen,
linked from MyRestaurant -> Manage -> Reviews.

StarRating is read-only by default and becomes tappable when handed an
onChange, so the same component does display and input.


Sample reviews

  python manage.py seed_reviews [--fresh]

94 restaurant reviews, 341 dish reviews. Each restaurant has a house
QUALITY between 3.4 and 4.8 and every rating is drawn around it, so the top
lists come out with a real spread instead of everything sitting at 4.5.
Signature and best-selling dishes get a small bonus. Roughly a third of
reviews deliberately have no comment.

The first version wrote a row at a time and timed out past two minutes -
the database is remote and that was ~1800 round trips. It now builds
everything in memory and writes with bulk_create + bulk_update. The
bulk_update is what makes a backdated created_at survive auto_now_add:
bulk_update reads the attribute straight off the instance instead of going
through pre_save. Same trick is reused by seed_messages.


Notifications

notification/queries.py: getNotifications (+ unread_count), getUnreadCount,
addNotification, markNotificationRead, markAllNotificationsRead,
deleteNotification. Everything is scoped by account_id, which is the whole
trick - the SAME endpoints serve owners and diners, and who you are decides
what comes back.

That carries into the UI: NotificationList holds the entire feed - load,
pull-to-refresh, optimistic mark-read, mark-all - so DinerNotificationScreen
and RestaurantNotificationScreen are ~20-line wrappers differing only in
navbar, empty-state wording and where a tap leads.

seed_reviews also seeds the diner side (owners get theirs from the reviews
themselves), so both roles have a populated feed: 131 notifications across
15 owners and 8 diners.


Home screens

ranking/queries.py: getTopRestaurants, getTopDishes, getNearbyRestaurants.
Ranked by a Bayesian score - MIN_REVIEWS = 3 pulls a low-count entry toward
the mean, so two 5-star reviews do not outrank forty 4.8s.

Distance is computed in SQL (haversine, clamped before acos because floats
can push the cosine past 1). Coordinates are stored as free text and are
inconsistent - the pre-existing row holds "~8.229° N" while the seeded ones
hold "8.2280" - so every read goes through
NULLIF(regexp_replace(col,'[^0-9.-]','','g'),'')::double precision. A row
that still will not parse drops out instead of failing the query.

IMPORTANT, changed mid-day on request: these lists were originally clipped
to a 40 km radius around the user. That was wrong for this app - every
seeded restaurant is in Northern Mindanao, so a device anywhere else saw
three empty sections, which is exactly what was reported. They now rank
across ALL of goodFood. Distance is still returned and shown on each card,
but it only narrows the list when radius_km is passed explicitly.
getNearbyRestaurants is the deliberate exception and keeps the 40 km
default, since "nearby" is its entire purpose.

Verified from Manila coordinates: 0/0/0 before the change, 3/3/3 after, and
identical to what Iligan coordinates return.

Both home screens now render the shared TopPicks: Top Dishes (2-up, rank
badge, average, review count, distance), Top Restaurants (3-up) and Latest
Reviews (real review cards; a tap opens the dish or the restaurant's review
list). Captions read "Highest rated on goodFood", not "around you".

Two UI details worth keeping:
- TopPicks renders each heading even while loading. The first version
  returned only a spinner, so during the fetch there was literally no "Top
  Dishes" heading on screen - which read as the section being missing.
- the "Near {city}" header label was removed. It was derived from the
  top-ranked result's city, which is meaningless once ranking is app-wide.
  There is no reverse geocoding available, so that slot now holds only a
  "Share your location to see distances" prompt when location is off.

The literal strings "Top Dishes" / "Top Restaurants" live ONLY in
TopPicks.js, not in either home screen file - that caused some confusion
when grepping the home screens for them.

DinerAssistedHomeScreen was deliberately left alone. It is a separate
Google-Places-style browser, not a top-picks home.

Follow-ups

- goodfood123 is committed in plaintext in seed_sample_data.py, and these
  accounts live in the REMOTE Postgres rather than a local throwaway. Anyone
  who can reach the API can log in as them. Fine for now; should become an
  env var or refuse to run when DEBUG is false before this goes anywhere.
- location_location.latitude/longitude should be normalised to numeric
  columns rather than sanitised on every read.
- ranking_restaurant_ranking / ranking_dish_ranking are still unused -
  rankings are computed live. Fine at this size; revisit if it gets slow.
- top lists are a pure global ranking. A location-weighted blend (great
  nearby food above great far-away food) may read better, and was left out
  only because "not around me" was the explicit ask.
